# Enterprise AI Use-Case & ROI Assessment Platform

## 1. Purpose

The platform evaluates a business process to determine:

1. Whether AI/automation is applicable
2. How much of the process can potentially be automated
3. The expected business impact
4. Technical feasibility
5. Data readiness
6. Implementation complexity
7. Risk and governance requirements
8. Recommended architecture
9. Estimated implementation and operating cost
10. Potential ROI and payback period

The output is a decision-support assessment, not an autonomous investment decision.

---

# 2. Assessment Dimensions

Each use case is evaluated across eight core dimensions.

| Dimension             | Score | Meaning                                                 |
| --------------------- | ----: | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Business Impact       | 0–100 | Potential value to the business                         |
| Automation Potential  | 0–100 | Amount of manual work that can potentially be automated |
| AI Applicability      | 0–100 | Suitability of AI for the problem                       |
| Technical Feasibility | 0–100 | Difficulty of implementing the proposed solution        |
| Data Readiness        | 0–100 | Availability and quality of required data               |
| Risk                  | 0–100 | Operational, compliance, accuracy and security risk     |
| Complexity            | 0–100 | Integration and implementation complexity               |
| Human Dependency      | 0–100 | Degree to which human involvement remains necessary     |

Higher scores are desirable for every dimension except Risk and Complexity.

# 3. Business Impact

Business Impact estimates the potential value generated by improving the process.

### Factors

| Factor                   | Weight |
| ------------------------ | -----: |
| Process Volume           |    25% |
| Time / Labor Consumption |    25% |
| Error / Rework Cost      |    20% |
| Financial Impact         |    20% |
| Strategic Importance     |    10% |

Each factor is normalized to 0–100.

### Interpretation

|  Score | Classification |
| -----: | -------------- |
|   0–39 | Low            |
|  40–69 | Medium         |
| 70–100 | High           |

# 4. Automation Potential

Automation Potential estimates the percentage of process activity that could potentially be automated.

The assessment considers:

* Repetitive tasks
* Rule-based decisions
* Structured inputs
* Document processing
* Classification
* Data extraction
* Search/retrieval
* Human judgment requirements
* Exception frequency

### Interpretation

|  Score | Classification |
| -----: | -------------- |
|   0–29 | Low            |
|  30–59 | Medium         |
|  60–79 | High           |
| 80–100 | Very High      |

Automation Potential is an estimate and should not be interpreted as guaranteed labor reduction.

# 5. AI Applicability

AI Applicability determines whether AI provides meaningful value compared with traditional software or rules.

### High AI applicability

Examples:

* Natural-language understanding
* Document interpretation
* Classification
* Information extraction
* Semantic search
* Unstructured data processing
* Complex pattern recognition
* Recommendation generation

### Lower AI applicability

Examples:

* Simple deterministic calculations
* Straightforward CRUD operations
* Fully deterministic business rules
* Processes with no meaningful decision or interpretation requirement

### Scoring factors

| Factor                     | Weight |

| Unstructured Data          |    20% |
| Reasoning / Interpretation |    20% |
| Pattern Recognition        |    15% |
| Language Requirement       |    15% |

| AI Advantage over Rules    |    15% |

# 10. Human Dependency

Human Dependency estimates how much human involvement remains necessary after automation.

Examples:

### Low

AI performs the process and humans only monitor exceptions.

### Medium

AI performs most processing but humans review selected cases.

### High

AI assists humans but does not independently execute important decisions.

Human Dependency should be determined based on:

* Decision criticality
* Error consequences
* Regulatory requirements
* Exception frequency
* Model confidence
* Need for human judgment

# 11. Overall Opportunity Score

The initial opportunity score combines business value, AI applicability and feasibility while accounting for risk and complexity.

### V1 Formula

```text
Opportunity Score =

Business Impact × 0.25
+ Automation Potential × 0.20
+ AI Applicability × 0.20
+ Technical Feasibility × 0.15
+ Data Readiness × 0.10
+ Human Automation Potential × 0.10

− Risk Penalty
− Complexity Penalty
```

For implementation, the normalized version will be:

```text
Positive Score =
0.25(Business Impact)
+ 0.20(Automation Potential)
+ 0.20(AI Applicability)
+ 0.15(Technical Feasibility)
+ 0.10(Data Readiness)
+ 0.10(Human Automation Potential)

Risk Penalty = Risk × 0.10

Complexity Penalty = Complexity × 0.05

Final Score =
Positive Score − Risk Penalty − Complexity Penalty
```

The exact weighting will be treated as configurable rather than universally correct.

# 13. Architecture Recommendation

The platform should recommend an architecture based on process characteristics.

### Rules Engine

Recommended when:

* Logic is deterministic
* Inputs are structured
* Decisions are explicitly defined
* Explainability is critical

### Traditional ML

Recommended when:

* Historical labeled data exists
* Prediction/classification is required
* Patterns are numerical or structured

### LLM

Recommended when:

* Natural language is involved
* Documents require interpretation
* Classification or extraction is required
* Reasoning over text is required

### RAG

Recommended when:

* The system needs enterprise-specific knowledge
* Internal documents are required
* Answers must be grounded in company information

### Agentic Architecture

Recommended when:

* Multiple tools/systems must be orchestrated
* The workflow contains multiple decision/action steps
* Dynamic task execution is required

### Fine-tuning

Recommended only when:

* A sufficiently large task-specific dataset exists
* Consistent specialized behavior is required
* Prompting/RAG is insufficient
* The expected benefit justifies training and maintenance cost

### Hybrid

Recommended when:

* Different parts of the process require different technologies
* Rules can handle deterministic validation
* AI handles unstructured interpretation
* Humans handle high-risk exceptions

---

# 14. Build vs Buy

The platform should evaluate:

### Build

Consider when:

* Process is highly specialized
* Existing products do not satisfy requirements
* Proprietary workflows create differentiation
* Internal data or IP is strategically important

### Buy

Consider when:

* Mature commercial solutions exist
* Requirements are standardized
* Speed to deployment is important
* Internal engineering capacity is limited

### Hybrid

Consider when:

* Core platform capabilities can be purchased
* Company-specific intelligence or workflow must be built internally

The platform should present the reasoning rather than simply returning "Build" or "Buy."

---

# 15. Human-in-the-Loop

The system should determine whether human review is required.

### Mandatory human review

Examples:

* High financial impact
* High regulatory impact
* High-risk decisions
* Low model confidence
* Significant consequences from errors

### Selective human review

Examples:

* AI handles normal cases
* Humans review exceptions
* Human review triggered by confidence thresholds

### Monitoring only

Possible when:

* Decisions are low risk
* Errors have limited consequences
* Process is highly reversible
* Model performance is demonstrably reliable

# 16. ROI Framework

The economics engine will calculate:

### Current Process Cost

```text
Current Labor Cost =
Monthly Volume × Processing Time × Hourly Labor Cost
```

Additional costs may include:

* Error/rework cost
* Software cost
* Operational overhead

### AI Solution Cost

```text
AI Cost =
Model/API Cost
+ Infrastructure Cost
+ Human Review Cost
+ Maintenance Cost
+ Development Cost Allocation
```

### Monthly Savings

```text
Monthly Savings =
Current Process Cost − AI Process Cost
```

### Annual Net Benefit

```text
Annual Net Benefit =
Annual Savings − Annual Operating Cost
```

### ROI

```text
ROI =
(Net Benefit − Implementation Cost)
/
Implementation Cost
× 100
```

### Payback Period

```text
Payback Period =
Implementation Cost
/
Monthly Net Savings
```

All financial calculations will clearly identify assumptions.

# 17. Scenario Analysis

The platform will support three scenarios:

### Conservative

* Lower automation
* Higher AI cost
* Higher human review
* Lower savings

### Expected

* Central assumptions

### Optimistic

* Higher automation
* Lower operating cost
* Lower human review
* Higher savings

The system should show how changes in assumptions affect ROI rather than presenting one precise number as a fact.
